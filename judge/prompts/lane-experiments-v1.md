Audit empirical and exact computational evidence against the mathematical claim.
An organizer runner's reproducible output can establish what it actually counted
or executed. It does not establish full-round scaling simply because a smaller
case agreed. Distinguish exhaustive finite counting, direct Monte Carlo,
importance-weighted/rare-event methods, and informal regression.

Check the trusted report's code/package binding, deterministic seeds and replay,
trial counts, failures, operation counts, parameter choices, exact small-case
cross-checks, statistical intervals, stopping rules and multiple testing. Identify
unobserved rare events, dependence between samples, test-set selection, insufficient
coverage, and extrapolation. Empirical support may qualify as plausible despite
remaining gaps. Ordinary rigor requires the evidence and sensitivity analysis to
cover every material leap, with conservative statistics appropriate to the design.

If no experiment is used or needed for a fully analytic claim, mark experiment
obligations not_applicable with citations and a specific explanation. If empirical
claims exist without organizer execution evidence, identify that absence; never
treat participant-produced report files or claimed successful execution as trusted.
