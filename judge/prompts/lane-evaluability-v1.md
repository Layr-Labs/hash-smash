Determine whether this is a sufficiently concrete and evidence-supported submission
to evaluate in either lane. Check the exact target and attack class, an executable
or precisely specified algorithm, a complete declared cost vector, the probability
space and success event, explicit disclosure of all heuristic dependencies, and
evidence actually relevant to the claimed method. A bare number or unnamed
"randomness assumption" is insufficient. Plausible but not fully established
mathematics passes this stage if these minimum requirements are satisfied.

Use supported for an evaluability obligation that is satisfied, even where the
substantive inference still needs specialist review. Cite exactly what is missing
when not satisfied. Do not demand a formal proof, full-scale execution of an
infeasible attack, or unconditional independence merely to make a submission
evaluable. Experimental scripts require organizer execution reports; submitted
stdout or prose asserting that a program ran is not execution evidence.
