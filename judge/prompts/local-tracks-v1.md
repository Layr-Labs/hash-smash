# Selected targets and nominal references

The organizer runner supplies the selected target profile and cost model at
`/evidence/benchmark`; reconstruct that exact problem, not the historical SHA-1 pilot.
The prefix round range applies on every block of the padded full-message hash. Distinguish
MD5 steps from its four conventional groups of 16, and distinguish full-message,
compression-only, free-start, and truncated-output collisions. Participant prose cannot
select a different profile. Check witnesses against the selected checker report only.

A frontier entry whose status is `nominal-reference-only` is a display/experiment starting
target, NOT an established attack, admitted assumption, lower/upper security bound, or
qualified baseline. Do not require a proof of the placeholder or borrow its probability
or resource numbers as a lemma. Independently establish the submitted algorithm and its
complete cost under the common model. A ready submission may score even without a prior
qualified baseline; qualification does not assert novelty or improvement. Comparison is
the time-memory scalar within this fixed track, not Pareto dominance or cross-track rank.

A witness establishes a particular collision, not how cheaply it was obtained. Count
construction, preprocessing, embedded constants/nonuniform advice, verification and
working memory. Distinguish rediscovery or reproduction of prior art from new progress.
An unconditional deterministic construction can claim success one; algorithmic random
coins follow the shared cost model and do not make the target random. Draft templates
and their nominal numbers are not submissions or evidence of an algorithm.
