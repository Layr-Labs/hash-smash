# SHA-256 reduced to 38 steps: ordinary collision by the P1 row-16 claw of the P8 characteristic, found by a distinguished-point walk in 2^20 bytes

**Target:** `sha256-r38-prefix-v1` (SHA-256 steps 0..37 on every padded block, standard IV, FIPS 180-4 padding, feed-forward, all eight digest words), lane `exploratory`.
**Claim:** `time_log2 = 89.3832` target-compressions (exact 89.38312, rounded up), `memory_log2_bytes = 20.0691` (a declared upper bound of 1,100,000 bytes: the enumerated worst case over the program's control flow, Section 6, is 1,045,840 bytes, and the remaining 54,160 bytes are a declared margin), success probability at least 0.39, `preprocessing_log2 = 78.09276` (the allowances of Section 9, charged inside the time), advice 260 bytes (`nonuniform_advice_log2_bytes = 8.03`).
**Scalar:** `hashsmash-scalar-tm-v1`: `89.3832 + max(0, 20.0691 - 20) = 89.4523` (memory penalty 0.0691 bit; the declared memory bound is above one MiB by its margin).

This entry takes the second-block construction, the row-16 claw and the downstream probabilities of newjordan's `51613d1c` (the P1 sum-claw on dariolina's P8 characteristic `1a695f0b`, Section 2) and finds the claws with the memory-bounded walk program of the r39 lineage (our `19034ce3`, lamb356 `4487063`, Jbenisek `d90878ff`, dkuthoore `0b2616e3`) instead of a 2^28-byte table. Everything the bound uses is defined, derived or counted in this file and in `experiments/r38joint.py` (own code, standard library; P8 is read from the public proof of `1a695f0b` as data, as `51613d1c` does; no code of another participant is executed).

## 1. Target and output

The program outputs two distinct 128-byte messages `m = M0 || M1` and `m' = M0 || M1'` with a common first block `M0`. FIPS padding appends the same third block to both.

**Lemma 1.** Let `cv = F_38(IV, M0)`. If the second-block compressions of `M1` and `M1'` from `cv` end with equal state words `A34..A37, E34..E37`, the digests of `m` and `m'` are equal. *Proof.* The second block's output is `cv + (A37, A36, A35, A34, E37, E36, E35, E34)` for both, and the third block is identical. The program compares the eight output words of the two second-block compressions exactly (`step3b`) and then recomputes both complete 3-block digests (`digest_r38`) before it outputs anything, so every output is a collision of the target; `M1 != M1'` because the two members differ in `W7` (`D7 = 0x20000000`).

## 2. The construction (credited prior work)

**P8 and the P1 pair (`1a695f0b`, `51613d1c` Section 2).** P8 is a 38-step semi-free-start characteristic with the published pair `(PCV, PMX, PMY)` (data in `experiments/r38joint.py` [1]). Member x of the P1 pair takes `A0 = a0`, `A1 = a1` free and `A2..A15`, `E6..E15` from the published trace of `PMX`; member y the same with the trace of `PMY`. For a chaining value `cv`, `E0..E5` and `W0..W15` of each member follow from the step equations (`variantW`); `W10..W15` are the published words and `W0..W9` depend on `(cv, a0, a1)`. Setting `cv = PCV`, `a0 = A0`, `a1 = A1` of the published pair returns `PMX` and `PMY` exactly (asserted at import).

**Row 16 is a claw (`51613d1c` Section 2).** With `u := W1 = a1 + h(a0, cv)`, the row-16 word of member x is
`E16x = F(a0) + P(u)`, `P(u) = s0(u) - u`, `F(a0) = a0 - S0(a0) - MAJ(a0, a, b) - S1(E0) - CH(E0, e, f) + KF`, `E0 = a0 + E0base`,
with `(a, b, e, f) = (cv[0], cv[1], cv[4], cv[5])`, `E0base = cv[3] - S0(a) - MAJ(a, b, cv[2])`, `KF = k0 + Cc2 - cv[6] - K[1]`, `k0 = -S0(a) - MAJ(a,b,cv[2]) - cv[7] - S1(e) - CH(e,f,cv[6]) - K[0]`, `Cc2 = 0x7af32e15`. These are the step-0 and step-1 equations written out; `selftest` and experiment `r38-p8-claw` (trials 1..255) check `F(a0) + P(u) = E16x` against the real step-0..16 trace of the P1 pair on random `(cv, a0, u)`.
The row-16 pass set of P8 is `S160 = { H_j + o : j < 20, o in OFFS }` with the 20 heads `R16H` (`H_j = H_0 + t_j 2^13`, bits 0, 2, 10 of every head zero) and `OFFS = {0, 1, 4, 5, 1024, 1025, 1028, 1029}` (bits 0, 2, 10 of the sum free). A **claw** is a pair `(a0, u)` with `E16x in S160`; equivalently, for the head `H_j`,
`(F(a0) - t_j 2^13) - (H_0 - P(u)) in OFFS`   (the program's `claw_core` test).

**What a claw still has to pay (`51613d1c` Sections 6, 7, 9).** A claw is a *good pair* when the four post-filters hold on the real words of the two members: `C1: W8y - W8x = D8`, `C2: s0(W9y) - s0(W9x) = D9s`, `G: s0(W8y) - s0(W8x) = T8`, `F7: W7y - W7x = D7 and s0(W7y) - s0(W7x) = F7d` (the five constants are the published pair's differences, recomputed at import). `51613d1c` counts them exactly: `q_pf = P(C1 C2 G F7 | claw) = 57,726,359,870,197 x 287,309,824 / 2^90 = 2^-16.1877` for `a0` uniform in the window `R = 9` (`a0 in [9 x 2^26, 10 x 2^26)`) and `a1` uniform. A good pair is accepted (its two second blocks collide) with probability `q3r = q3_P1 x 2^32 / 160 = 2^-71.4010`, where `q3_P1 = 2^-96.0791 = 2^-96.0133 x 2^-0.0658` is the registered accepted-event rate of the P1 family on window `R = 9` (the A0-variant base over 128 + 400 seeds and the paired P1-minus-A0 difference, one-sided 99% lower bound `D_LB = -0.0658` over 36 seeds; `51613d1c` Section 9 and Appendices P, Q). Together one claw yields a collision with probability `q_pf x q3r = 2^-87.5887` at the exact rates. This filing does not charge the exact post-filter rates: Section 5 measures G and F7 on this program's own claws over a fresh holdout set and charges each at min(exact, its one-sided 99% Wilson lower bound on distinct claws), giving `q_pf = 2^-16.273626` and `p_claw = 2^-87.674654` (Section 7); the inherited `q3r` is a cited transfer premise (Section 9).

## 3. The walk and the program

**Points.** A point is a 29-bit word `v`. Let `r(y)` be the 32-bit word `y` with bits 0, 2 and 10 removed (29 bits). The walk step is
- side `s = bit 28 of v`. **A side** (`s = 0`): head index `j = bits 26..27` (one of the window's four heads `heads4`, Section 3 below), `a0 = (9 << 26) | bits 0..25` (window `R = 9`), image `y = F(a0) - t_{heads4[j]} 2^13`;
- **B side** (`s = 1`): `u = v' | mix4(v') << 28` with `v' = bits 0..27` and `mix4 = (v' >> 3) xor (v' >> 11) xor (v' >> 19) xor (v' >> 24) mod 16`, image `y = H_0 - P(u)`;
- next point `v_next = r(y)`; `v_next` is a distinguished point (DP) iff its low 6 bits are zero.

**Lemma 2 (what a cross-side collision is).** Let `x` be an A point and `y` a B point with `r(y_A) = r(y_B)`. Then `y_A xor y_B` lies in the span of `{1, 4, 1024}`, so `d = y_A - y_B = sum_k 2^k (a_k - b_k)` over `k in {0, 2, 10}` with `a_k, b_k` the free bits of the two images, and `(a0, u)` is a claw with head `heads4[j]` iff `d in OFFS` iff `a_k >= b_k` for all three `k`. The program tests `d` exactly on every cross-side collision (`claw_core`: `d & ~0x405 == 0`); the collisions that fail it (`notclaw`) are discarded. Under uniform free bits the pass probability is `(3/4)^3 = 27/64 = 0.4219` (measured 0.4215 over the 24 windows). No equality test on a quotient of the word does better than 3/4 per free bit, and the 29-bit quotient is the best choice among quotienting 0..3 of the free bits (DESIGN record; it is a design choice, not a premise: the program tests `d` exactly).

**Heads.** The 20 heads are partitioned into five subsets of four, `SUBSETS[s] = {4s, .., 4s+3}`. The attack (`--attack`) cycles the subsets, window `w` using `(start + w) mod 5` with `start` drawn once from the system's random source, so over any five consecutive windows every head is used once; with the per-subset claw yields equal (Section 5: the five holdout subset means of distinct groups per window agree within 0.66%, the sampling error of 4-6-window means), the attack-wide mixture of the claws over the 160 row-16 passes is uniform, which is the mixture the inherited rates are pooled over. The measured windows use the same rule with `start = B mod 5` of their seed base `B` (`--windows`), so the 24 evaluation windows and the 48 G windows sample all five subsets (Section 5 reports the yield per subset and charges the subset-stratified bound). The A side of the point space (2^28 points) is then `(a0 in R = 9) x (4 heads)`, balanced against the 2^28 B points (cross-side share of collisions measured 0.4999).

**Why a0 stays in window R = 9.** `q_pf` is an exact count for `R = 9` and `q3_P1` is measured on `R = 9` (`51613d1c` refused to widen the window until it had measured the transfer, its A.23-A.28). The head bits enlarge the A side instead.

**The program (`experiments/r38joint.py`, sections [3]-[4]).** Per first block `M0` the program runs one WINDOW of `2^25` walk evaluations (`Attack.run_window`):
1. Two random 256-bit words give `M0` (16 words, 18 operations); one target compression gives `cv`; the constants of `F` (140) and of the post-filter chain (260).
2. The generator (`build_kernel`, `allocate`, `to_source`, section [2]) emits the two kernel images (bank A to bank B and B to A) of the 256-lane step: one record = XOR, AND, OR or NOT of 256-bit registers (bit `j` of the 256 points lives in plane `j`; rotations are renaming; constants of `cv` and of the four heads are folded; MAJ and CH with constant operands reduce to wires; a `MUX` selects the A or B image per lane; the DP plane is the OR of the six low output planes). The images have 1199 to 1254 records over the 24 windows and the organizer's PCV image (cap `KCAP = 1300`) and use at most 158 registers (cap 240; 64 fixed: two banks of 32 planes, the DP plane at 64). The generator is charged **at the cap `GCAP = 2^21 = 2,097,152` operations per window**, in `m.o` (Section 4). Verification: `r38-p8-claw` trial 0 (organizer sandbox) and `selftest` execute both images on 256 random lanes against the scalar step (0 mismatches, DP plane included); check mode asserts every claw found by the walk against the scalar maps.
3. The table is cleared (4 operations per word): `WORDS = 27,648` words of 32 bytes, six entries of 40 bits each; 256 trails start in the 256 lanes, trail `i` at point `i`.
4. Stage 1 (`Attack.iter1`): `2^17` iterations of the kernel; on a DP (`Attack.hit`): the lane is found (binary search), the 23 high bits `r` of the point are extracted (Horner over the planes), the word is `r x 27,648 >> 23` (four shifts and three adds), the check field is `chk = r mod 512` (the `r` values of one word form an interval of at most 304 values, so `(word, chk)` determines `r`: no false match). The six entries of the word are scanned: an entry with the same `chk` and a nonzero length is a MATCH (two trails ended at this DP): the pair `(id, len)` of both trails goes to a queue of 4096 pairs, and the stored entry is replaced only if the new trail is longer (keep-longer, dkuthoore `0b2616e3`); otherwise the DP is written to an empty entry of the word, or, if the word is full, to the entry selected by a rotating counter. The lane receives a new trail (next id, `insert_lane`). A trail older than 1280 iterations is cut by a scan every 512 iterations.
5. Stage 2 (`Attack.stage2`) when the queue holds 3840 pairs and at the end of the window: pairs run in lane pairs `(p, p + 128)`, 128 slots, continuous refill; the longer trail starts first and the shorter `len` difference iterations later (timing wheel of 2048 buckets); after each iteration the two states are compared (12 planes, then the other 17 for candidates); the first equal state gives the predecessors `(x, y)`; `x = y` (the shorter trail started on the longer one) is discarded (`degenerate`).
6. The claw (`Attack.claw`, `claw_core`, every operation counted): sides from bit 28 (same side: discarded, `same`); decode `(a0, j)` and `u`; `F(a0)`, `P(u)`, the exact test of Lemma 2 (`notclaw` on failure); `a1 = u - F + a0 + K2` (the claw equation); `C1` on `W8x, W8y`; `C2` on `W9`; `G`; `F7` (the words from the closed forms of the step equations, asserted against `variantW` in check mode); step 3b: both second blocks `W0..W15` from `(cv, a0, a1)`, two 38-step compressions (2 x 2728 charged), exact comparison of the eight words. On acceptance the two 3-block digests are recomputed (6 x 2728 + 1600 charged) and the messages are output.

The attack is the loop over first blocks (`--attack`): the 16 words of each window's first block are drawn from the operating system's random source (fresh algorithmic coins; `--seed` exists only for the measurements and experiments). Halts: a success, the window count `B_WINDOWS`, the charged-work budget `W_BUDGET` (Section 7), and a window ends when its trail counter `idc` reaches `2^20` (it peaks at 526,959 over the 24 windows).

## 4. Machine model and prices (declared once)

Counted 256-bit word RAM with 256 registers (8,192 bytes, counted), cost model `collision-frontier-v5`, unit the target compression = 2728 operations (`C` of `sha256-r38`). One operation each: XOR, AND, OR, NOT, move, shift, rotate, add, subtract, compare-and-set; a conditional branch is a compare and a branch (2); a load or a store is 3 (index shift, base add, access); a call and its return are 2. A kernel record (register-to-register XOR/AND/OR/NOT of 256-bit words) is one operation and is stored in 5 bytes (opcode and three 10-bit register fields; the price of `6b678689`, `0c1ef7c3`, `19034ce3`, `0b2616e3`). Every word operation of the program passes through the class `Mach` (one method = one charged primitive; `m.o` counts them); the kernel is charged `len(records)` per iteration; the program's own control flow that is not a `Mach` call (the six-entry scan loop and its flag tests, the keep-longer chain, the per-iteration cut-scan and halt tests, the initialisation of a stage-2 phase) is charged by explicit allowances in `m.o`: `CF_HIT = 16` per DP hit (an instrumented count found 14.3-15.7 decisions), `CF_ITER = 4` per iteration, `CF_PHASE = 1024` per stage-2 phase; the generator is charged the cap `GCAP = 2^21` per window (its own builder-call census, 2,870 calls at most over the 24 windows, each a handful of Python operations, is far below the cap; the cap is the charge). Memory is the inventory of Section 6, with the code charged as the full byte length of the shipped program. These are prices of the cost model, not probabilistic premises.

## 7. Success probability and time

`p_claw = q_pf x q3r = 2^-87.674654` with the charged `q_pf = 2^-16.273626` (Section 5) and `q3r = 2^-71.4010` (Section 2; Section 9 for the premises). **Claws are counted by groups.** Two claws of a window can share a trial only through a shared endpoint (`a0` or `u`); grouping the distinct claws of a window into connected components by shared endpoint (`count_groups`), one claw per group is one trial of the accepted event, and distinct groups have disjoint inputs `(a0, a1)`. Conditional on at least `G` distinct groups, the run succeeds with probability at least `1 - exp(-G p_claw)`. Set `G p_claw = lambda = -ln(0.60999) = 0.494313`, so `1 - exp(-lambda) = 0.390010` and `log2 G = 86.658150` (`p_claw = 2^-87.674654` from the charged `q_pf = 2^-16.273626` of Section 5 with the unrounded Wilson bound 0.0158395 and the exact rational `G | C1 C2`; all figures here are printed to the precision the ceiling needs). With the lower bound `g- = 35,841.65` groups per window and the margin `(1 + 2^-19)`,
`B = ceil(G (1 + 2^-19) / g-) = 3406554978111845260346 = 2^71.5288` windows (first blocks), where `g- = 35,841.65` is the lower of the pooled/per-set bound (35,922.7) and the smallest head-subset bound (35841.6; Section 5); `B` is the integer the shipped program holds (`B_WINDOWS`); it is the ceiling of the unrounded chain, and the printed 6-7-digit figures reproduce it to within 3 x 10^-5 relative (the success probability at this `B` is 0.3900099 and `log2 T` is unchanged at 4 decimals).

**Total work (Bernstein) and total groups (Hoeffding).** The windows are independent trials (fresh coins per window, H1 part (a)), each with charged work `W_j in [0, R]` and distinct groups in `[0, 2^25]`; `E[W_j] = mu <= mu+` and `E[groups] = g >= g-` is premise H1 part (b). Since `0 <= W_j <= R`, `Var(W_j) <= E[W_j^2] <= R mu <= R mu+`, and Bernstein's inequality with `t = 2^-20 B mu+` gives: the total work of `B` windows exceeds `B mu+ (1 + 2^-20)` with probability at most `exp(-t^2 / (2 (B R mu+ + R t / 3))) = exp(-2^-40 B mu+ / (2 R (1 + 2^-20/3))) = exp(-2^6.8)` (the leader's Lemma 3 form; a plain Hoeffding bound with `R = 2^46` would be vacuous at this `B`). By Hoeffding's inequality the total number of distinct groups falls below `B g- (1 - 2^-20) >= G` with probability at most `exp(-2 B (g- 2^-20)^2 / 2^50) = exp(-2^12.8)`.

**The budget.** `--attack` stops at a success, after `B_WINDOWS = B` windows, or as soon as its charged work reaches `W_BUDGET = floor(B mu+ (1 + 2^-20)) + 1 = 2201255021173372656305624422107` operations (checked before each window, 6 operations per window), whichever comes first; so its charged work is at most `W_BUDGET + R + 6 B` in every run. The time is
`T = (W_BUDGET + R + 6 B) / 2728 + 2^78.09276` target-compressions (the second term is the preprocessing allowance of Section 9, 0.0399% of the total), **`log2 T = 89.38312`, `time_log2 = 89.3832`** (rounded up; the plain means give 89.3442). By the union bound the unconditional success probability is at least `0.390010 - exp(-2^6.8) - exp(-2^12.8) > 0.39`.

Sensitivity: every bit of `log2 p_claw` moves `time_log2` one for one (Section 9 lists what each inherited number is and its registered margin); each +10% of operations per distinct claw is +0.138 bit.
